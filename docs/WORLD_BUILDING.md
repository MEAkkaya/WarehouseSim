# Placing environment objects (collision only, no interaction)

## The short answer
**Make one small scene per object. Do NOT write a script class for it.**

A prop that only has a shape and a collider needs no code. A scene is enough, and
it stays editable in the editor:

```
Bench (StaticBody3D)            <- no script
├─ Model          (bench.glb)   <- your Blender model, instanced
└─ CollisionShape3D             <- a simple box/capsule, NOT the model's mesh
```

Save it as `scenes/props/world/bench.tscn`, then drag that scene into the level as
many times as you like. Change the bench once, every copy changes.

Add a script only when the object DOES something (opens, animates, can be picked
up). Then it gets a class (`class_name`), like Box or Shelf.

## Collision tips
- Use simple shapes (box, capsule, a few boxes for an L-shaped building), never the
  full mesh. It is faster and the player will not catch on small details.
- Let Blender make them: name an extra mesh `Bench-col` (box collider) or
  `Bench-convcol` (convex) and Godot's glTF import builds the StaticBody3D for you.
  `Bench-colonly` makes an invisible collider only.
- Put props on collision layer 1 ("world"), like the walls and the floor.

## Where to put them
Hand-placed props go under `Town/Props` in `scenes/levels/world.tscn` - the town is part of
that one scene (or into a room scene for the interior). Group them in sub-nodes (`Props/Street`,
`Props/Factory`) so the Scene tree stays readable.

## Many copies of the same thing (trees, fences, houses)
Hundreds of scene instances are slow. For mass placement use a MultiMesh: many copies
of one mesh drawn in one call, split into ~60 m chunks that are hidden when far away.
`scripts/world/town_builder.gd` does exactly this for the grey-box houses, sidewalks and
vegetation - copy that approach (or swap its mesh for your own model's mesh).
Fewer than ~50 of something (a building, a road): just put them in the scene.

## Far away things (LOD) and the world edge
- Distance fog (in the World Environment, colour follows the sky) hides pop-in and
  the world edge.
- `visibility_range_end` on a node hides it beyond a distance; for models with a
  low-poly version, `visibility_range_begin/end` on two nodes swaps them (manual LOD).
- Invisible walls close the playable area (see `_build_border` in the builder).


## Editing the warehouse layout
- Walls between rooms: select the `CSGBox3D` nodes in `scenes/levels/warehouse.tscn` (Position / Size in the Inspector,
  Ctrl+D to duplicate; keep "Use Collision" on). Outer walls, hall, office and the roof: `scenes/levels/world.tscn` ->
  `WarehouseShell`.
- Shelf areas are generated by the `StorageRegion` nodes (Z and X are turned 90 degrees: their corridors run east-west).
  Change `corridor_count`, `columns_per_side`, `levels` in the Inspector.
- After moving walls, update `scripts/world/warehouse_layout.gd`: the supervisors' route, the co-workers' loops, the work
  floor rectangles and the lamp rooms are listed there.

- The big layout was generated by a script from a handful of numbers (rooms, doors, region origins). Moving a wall by hand is fine;
  for larger changes ask for a regeneration. Region aisles: `aisle_width` (2.6 m), the Z/X rooms need `4 x (aisle + 2 x 1.0)` of depth.
- Signs: use `PaperLabel` (or `ApartmentKit.label`) instead of Label3D.


## Editing the town (one scene: `world.tscn` -> `Town`)
- **Houses and apartment blocks**: `Town/Buildings/Houses/House01`.. / `Apartment01`.. (a StaticBody3D with `Walls`, `Roof`, a box
  collider; apartments also `Shopfront`, `Windows1..`, `Sign`). Customise them one by one: move the node, change the meshes, or
  delete the grey boxes and drop in your own `.glb` + collider.
- **Move a building**: select it (e.g. `Town/Buildings/Shops/Shop3`) and change its Position; resize it in the Inspector
  (its `Mesh` BoxMesh size and the `Collision` box size). Replace a grey box by your own model: delete the box, drag your
  `.glb` into the same parent, add a `StaticBody3D` collider. The sidewalks and vegetation keep away from every MeshInstance3D under
  `Buildings` / `Props` by themselves.
- **Roads / railway**: `Town/Roads/*` are `TownRoad` nodes (Path3D). Drag the curve points in the 3D view (only x/z count, keep
  y = 0), add points with Ctrl+click; `width` and `lift` in the Inspector. Kind RAIL draws the railway. Sidewalks and the
  houses follow the roads at game start. The strip is drawn in the editor too (the script is a `@tool`).
- **Lakes**: `Town/Lakes/*` (`TownLake`): Position = centre, Scale x / z = the radii (metres) of the water's edge. In the editor
  a lake is only a flat blue disc (the handle); at game start it is dug into a basin (bank, bed, deep water shader) and the
  ground slab gets a hole there. The sand of the beach lake is the disc `BeachSand` next to it (centre and radii = the sand patch;
  it is replaced by the sand of the bank). The fence round the shore is generated from the lake. Keep roads out of the lake
  (a road strip over the hole would float over the water): a road may end at the bank.
- **Ground**: `Town/Ground` is one big slab; the generator cuts the night club's stair shaft into it at game start.
  `Town/IndustrialPaving` and `Town/Fields/*` are flat boxes (the generator treats them as "not free ground").
- **Apartment blocks** (`Buildings/GamblingApartment`, `ClubApartment`): their rooms are built in code when the game starts, so the
  editor only shows an orange see-through box (group `editor_only`). Moving the node moves the whole building (and the map
  marker, and the club's ground hole).
- Still numbers in `scripts/world/town_layout.gd`: the playable area (`BOUNDS`), the neighbourhood zone, the woods, `KEEP_OUT`
  (the neighbourhood's houses stay out of those rectangles: update `KEEP_OUT` when you rebuild near the warehouse).
- Group `editor_only`: any node in it is deleted at game start (use it for reference boxes).

## Characters
- Every person (co-workers, supervisors, the black market dealer) uses `assets/models/employee.glb` through
  `scenes/npc/employee_model.tscn` (script `CharacterModel`). The glb faces +Z (the glTF way); the wrapper scene turns it 180 degrees
  and centres it, so people look along -Z like everything else. Origin = feet, 1.75 m tall.
- Look: `assets/textures/employeeUv.png` is the painted body; `assets/textures/employeeUv_mask.png` is black except on the clothes, where it
  holds the brightness of the base texture. `assets/shaders/character_tint.gdshader` replaces the clothes colour with `shirt_color` /
  `pants_color` (exports of `CharacterModel`), shaded by the mask so folds and patterns stay; skin, face and hair stay as painted.
  Shirt and trousers are told apart by their place in the texture (`split_v` = 0.6: shirt islands rows 103-152, trousers rows 154-193 of
  256). If you move the islands, change `split_v` and the two `*_average` uniforms. Co-workers get a shirt colour from their name.
- The glb is now one skinned mesh (`Char`, 25 bones) with two clips, `idle` and `walk` (looping, set in code). `CharacterModel`
  watches how fast it moves in the world and plays `walk` (speed scaled by `speed / stride_speed`, 1.25 m/s = no foot sliding) or
  `idle` (random start offset), blending 0.2 s, so the NPC scripts do not know about animation.
- The `walk` clip's last key equals its first, so the loop shows one repeated frame (about 0.04 s).
